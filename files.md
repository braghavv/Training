LC document Checking Agent
Process Flow

1. Document Intake
Upload PDF, scanned LC, SWIFT MT700, amendments and supporting documents
2. OCR and Document Classification
Extract text, detect document type, identify pages and preserve source references
3. LC Data Extraction
Extract LC number, parties, amount, currency, dates, shipment terms and document requirements
4. Normalize and Consolidate
Convert extracted data into a standard LC schema and apply amendments in sequence
5. Validation and Rule Engine
Check mandatory fields, dates, amounts, document clauses and cross-document consistency
6. Discrepancy Detection
Identify missing, inconsistent, ambiguous or potentially non-compliant information
7. Human Review and Approval
Trade operations reviews evidence, resolves exceptions and approves the result




General Problem Statement
Letter of Credit (LC) document checking is a highly manual, time-consuming, and error-prone process that requires trade operations staff to review multiple document types, interpret complex LC terms and amendments, validate cross-document consistency, and identify discrepancies. The lack of automated extraction, standardization, and validation increases operational risk, processing time, compliance exposure, and the possibility of overlooking material discrepancies, ultimately impacting turnaround time and service quality.
AI/OCR-Based Solution Oriented
Trade operations teams receive LCs, amendments, SWIFT messages, and supporting documents in various formats and layouts. Manual extraction and verification of critical LC information across multiple documents is inefficient and susceptible to human error. There is a need for an intelligent document checking solution that can automatically extract, normalize, validate, and compare LC data while highlighting discrepancies and exceptions for human review, thereby improving accuracy, compliance, and operational efficiency.
Banking Operations Focus
The current LC document scrutiny process relies heavily on manual document examination, resulting in high processing effort, inconsistent interpretation of LC clauses, delayed discrepancy detection, and increased operational risk. As transaction volumes grow, maintaining accuracy, regulatory compliance, and turnaround times becomes increasingly challenging without an automated mechanism for document ingestion, data extraction, validation, and discrepancy identification.
Problem Statement for BRD/Project Proposal
To address the increasing volume and complexity of documentary credit transactions, the bank requires an automated LC Document Checking Solution capable of processing scanned trade documents, extracting key LC information, applying amendments, validating compliance against LC terms and banking rules, detecting discrepancies, and providing auditable evidence for reviewer approval. The objective is to reduce manual effort, improve accuracy, strengthen compliance, and accelerate document processing while maintaining human oversight for exception handling.
One-Line Executive Version
"Manual LC document checking is resource-intensive, error-prone, and difficult to scale, creating a need for an automated solution that can extract, validate, and reconcile trade document data while identifying discrepancies and ensuring compliance."
